How the numbers are made

Stock baselines
The stock Levo 4 and Levo 4 EVO numbers come straight from Specialized's user manual. A setup that matches one exactly is tagged "Specialized chart." Everything else is calculated from there.
Switching the stock bike changes only what you're compared against; your settings stay. Use Reset to stock to load that bike's stock settings. A Levo 4 with the EVO extension is compared against the stock Levo 4.
Chips and cups
From the manual's adjustment table. Horst Long: +11 mm chainstay, −12 mm BB, −0.8°. High shock chip: −2 mm chainstay, +6 mm BB, +0.4°. Headset cup: ±1°, ±2 mm BB. The EVO extension has no High/Low chip.
Fork length
A longer fork tips the whole frame back about 0.4° per 10 mm of axle-to-crown, so reach, stack and seat angle rotate with it. With the EVO link and a 597 mm fork, this reproduces Specialized's EVO chart within about 1 mm.
Forks
Axle-to-crown comes from each maker's spec sheet: Fox 36 (2023–27), Fox 38 (2021–26 and 2027), and RockShox ZEB. Travel is limited to what each fork comes in. The same travel isn't the same height: at 160 mm the 2021–26 38 is 573.7 mm and the 2027 38 is 578.6 mm.
Specialized's charts use one nominal length, 577 mm at 160 and 597 mm at 180, for every trim. The stock comparisons use that. Pick Custom to enter any fork's A2C and offset.
Headset cup and reach
Reach doesn't change. The cup tips the frame slightly, which moves the head tube back about 3 mm on a +1° cup, but the steerer leans about 2 mm forward, so the stem barely moves.
Stack and seat angle do change: about +2 mm and −0.25° on a +1° cup, the reverse on −1°.
Estimates
Wheelbase, front center and trail are calculated. The manual lists chainstay changes but not wheelbase, so wheelbase after a chip change is an estimate.
Limits
Specialized rates the frame for forks up to 180 mm. The EVO extension needs a 230×62.5 standard-eye shock; a 230×65 isn't supported.
Marketing lists the long chainstay as 444 mm; the manual says +11 mm (446). This follows the manual.
